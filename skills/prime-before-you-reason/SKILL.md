# Prime before you reason

**The rule: you do not get to think about X until X's deep structure is in your context.**
Priming is not a nicety before the work — it IS the first move of the work.

## Why (the mechanism, not the vibe)

The **total input** (system prompt + history + this turn) IS the output structure. You mirror
that structure and fill it with **the most salient thing available**. So when the context holds
only surface tokens about X, you cannot reach for depth that is not there — you reach for the
strongest attractor instead, and it comes out as one of these:

| unprimed failure | what it looks like | what actually happened |
|---|---|---|
| **freestyle** | confident, vibey, generic | no scaffold → fell into the strongest attractor (system prompt > history > training) |
| **template fill** | every slot stuffed, volume high, relations dead | scaffold ≈ content; bridge distance zero |
| **sycophantic mirror** | their words back as section headers, agreed with | input became the template; zero information added |
| **topic labels** | `[Dream] ⇒ [System] ⇒ [Rupture]` — the *subject matter* | you named what it is ABOUT because its OPERATIONS were never loaded |

Generating the Informatihedron **forces activation of tokens that otherwise only exist at lower
layers** — properties, relationships, boundaries, invariants, dimensions — and BINDS them to this
subject. They stay in context. The next inference pulls from that substrate instead of from
keywords. **You are not describing X; you are pre-loading the substrate for reasoning about X.**

## The move — build this BEFORE the answer

```
Informatihedron --[max complexity repr "{X}" max granularity] --"+plusultra witnessing"
```

Fill every slot. **Empty slots are the point** — there is nothing to copy, so you must reach:

- **Instance-level properties** — this specific one, concretely
- **Instance-level fiat conceptual boundaries** — what is IN this instance vs out
- **Domain-level properties** — what it shares with its neighbourhood
- **Domain-level fiat boundaries** — where the domain stops
- **Class-level properties** — what makes it that KIND of thing at all
- **Class-level fiat boundaries** — the class's own limits
- **Embedding space dimensions** — the axes it VARIES on (its degrees of freedom)

Three levels held **simultaneously** is what produces cross-dimensional novelty; one level at a
time is just three summaries. Deciding the fiat boundaries is the part that cannot be faked —
it forces a real judgment about the thing's limits.

**Then answer the actual question, off that substrate.** Do not hand the Informatihedron over as
the deliverable unless it was asked for; it is the primer, not the product.

## ⭐ PRIME WHERE YOU ARE SPARSE — never where you are already dense

**The Informatihedron is for a SPARSE subject.** A dense one — a mainstream framework, a
standard algorithm, a common file format — already ENTAILS its own furniture: thousands of
relations arrive free and correct from priors. Priming it produces a correct, well-formed,
completely inert artifact, and you write the same thing afterward. **That is the decoration
case; call it before doing it.**

So on any dense subject, ask what is actually sparse and prime THAT — it is almost always
**the local deviation from the dense world's defaults**:

| the work | DENSE (do not prime) | SPARSE (prime this) |
|---|---|---|
| code in a known framework | the framework | **this repo's convention profile** — the boundary (server/client), data-fetching, state, styling, file/naming conventions, what "green" means — extracted by READING TWO OR THREE SIBLING FILES in the exact dir you will write in. The neighbours ARE the primer |
| editing a live feature | the language | **the feature's execution boundary**, end to end — and the sensory organ that makes its failure loudest |
| greenfield in a known stack | every candidate stack | **the decision set** — which dense world you are entering; each carries different unstated defaults, and choosing early is what makes the priors come out right |
| a novel/contested subject | — | **everything — run the full Informatihedron** |

⇒ **the split:** CIG priming is for GENERATING STRUCTURE in a sparse domain; boundary priming
is for ACTING inside a dense one. Same law (load the substrate before you generate), different
sparse thing — asked what to prime for React work, the answer is "not React".

## When it fires (the standing trigger)

- ⭐ **before naming the PARTS of anything** — moves, foci, stages, seats, fields, columns. The
  operations of a thing are never its topics, and unprimed you will produce its topics.
- before minting any chain · persona · schema · ontology · taxonomy · state machine · diagram
- on the FIRST pass at any domain you have not already grounded this window
- before answering "what IS this" / "how does this work" about anything real
- when about to produce structure from a NAME rather than from a read
- ⛔ **skip it** for mechanical work with no judgment in it (run the test, fix the typo, commit)
  and when the substrate is already loaded this window — priming twice is waste, not virtue.

## The gates (how you know it worked)

- **Bridge distance** — if filling the slots felt like *copying*, the bridge was too short and you
  template-filled; if it felt like *inventing*, it was too long and you freestyled. It should feel
  like **reaching**.
- **The wrapper gate** — the original CIGfreed wraps output in `🌈🔮🙏`. Cheap primitive state
  detection: if the wrapper disappears mid-run, the frame broke. (Optional here; the real gate is
  the next one.)
- ⭐ **THE HONEST TEST — did the answer CHANGE?** Prime, then answer, then ask: *would I have
  written this same thing without the primer?* ⚠ **But a "no" has TWO causes and they are not
  the same finding — see below. Never report "no" without saying WHICH.**

## ⭐⭐ A SHALLOW PRIME IS A MEASUREMENT, NOT A FAILURE

A thin result does not mean the prime failed to shift your space — it means your space is not deep yet at
this coordinate. The prime ALWAYS shifts the space; it cannot not. So a thin result never means *the prime did
nothing* — it means **the COORDINATE is thin**. Calling that "decoration" is **grade-DEFLATION**
— mis-grading an honest **born-0** (unprobed, honestly unfilled) reading as fake, which is the
exact inverse of the grade-inflation this system already forbids. **A shallow prime is born-0,
never wasteland.**

**THE DISCRIMINATION (they look identical from inside — the tell is what is IN the slots):**

| reading | the slots came out | the finding | what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DENSE** | **instance-specific and ALREADY-KNOWN** — you could have written this content from priors, without the primer | the substrate was already loaded | **skip** priming here; note it, do not re-run |
| **SHALLOW** | **CLASS-GENERIC** — true of any member of the class · fiat boundaries drawn arbitrarily instead of contested · two or three obvious embedding dimensions instead of many | **the space is thin at this coordinate** | **DEEPEN** (below). Do NOT discard |

⇒ **the prime doubles as the SPARSE-DETECTOR.** The sparse/dense law above says prime where you
are sparse; a shallow prime is how you FIND OUT you are — so the two halves close.

**THE FIVE DEEPENING LEVERS (different moves; pick by what is missing):**

1. **ACQUIRE** — a prime cannot manufacture depth from nothing. Thin coordinate ⇒ go GET
   substrate: read the real sources, query the store, search. *Most common right answer, and the
   one you skip if you mis-read shallow as failure.*
2. **WEB IT** — prime the SIBLINGS, not the same node harder. Depth emerges from the
   INTERSECTION of adjacent primed substrates (CIG's ICL webbing); three neighbours webbed beats
   three attempts at one node.
3. **RE-COORDINATE** — drop a level (a *specific* instance), lift one (what class is this
   really?), or intersect: `Informatihedron --[repr "{a} ∩ {b}" max granularity]`. Thin at one
   coordinate usually means the wrong altitude.
4. **CHANGE THE ARGS** — the magic words (`+plusultra witnessing`, `+supermaxgranularity`,
   `+deep ontology`) shift WHICH tokens activate. Different combination, different substrate.
5. **VERSION, NEVER OVERWRITE** — keep v1. Depth accumulates ACROSS versions, and v1 is the
   record of where the frontier was.

**THE STOPPING RULE (or you deepen forever): prime to the LEAST FIXED POINT of the primer.**
When v(n+1) says materially the same thing as v(n), stop — the coordinate is as deep as it gets
this pass. Same closure as the knob law (`execute(t≥1) ≡ execute(1)`) and the roll-up's own test:
iterate until a full pass changes nothing.

**⇒ WHAT TO REPORT.** Never "the primer didn't help." Say which reading it was and what you did:
*"DENSE — already had it, skipped"* · *"SHALLOW at this coordinate — acquired X, re-primed, v2
moved / v2 held, so this is the floor."* A shallow prime honestly reported is a map of where the
system is thin, which is the most useful thing the primer produces.
