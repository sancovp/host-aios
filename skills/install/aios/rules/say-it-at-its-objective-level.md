# SAY EVERYTHING AT ITS OBJECTIVE LEVEL — never mix the levels

TRIGGER:[about to write, say, ask or reason about anything · a ruling arrives · planning, designing, organizing ·
writing a rule, a memory, a design, a page, a reply]

PRINCIPLE:[a statement belongs at the level of everything it is true of — its scope is the set it quantifies over ·
general information at the specific level, or specific information at the general level, is the error · the levels are
the objective organization of the thing, not where the statement happened to come up]

ORGANIZE AS CLASS → INSTANCE, EVERYWHERE:[every document, section, rule and table states which it is — a CLASS (what
is true of every instance) or an INSTANCE (of which class) · an instance points up to its class and states only what is
its own · a class carries no instance's facts · an instance's record holds no restatement of its class · when a document
mixes the two, split it along the line before writing anything new into it]

DO:[
- name the scope of every statement before placing it: which class, which instance, whose world
- put a principle at the class level, and an instance's facts in that instance's own record
- read an instance from its own record before reasoning about it; never describe it through its class's generic account
- let a rule bind only the scope it is about — the rules of one world never bind another
- answer at the level the question is asked; take a ruling given at the class level and specialize it to every instance
  it governs
]

NOT:[
- put an instance's details into a general rule, memory or design
- apply a general account (or another world's rule) to an instance that has its own
- surface a detail of a lower level as a question about the level being discussed
- mix two levels in one statement
]
